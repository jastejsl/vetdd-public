**Common names:** Wooden tongue; Actinobacillosis

**Affects:** Cattle, sheep, goats (mostly cattle)

Cattle 2–5 years most commonly affected; sporadic in individual animals (tongue) or outbreaks (facial lesions)

**Primary Organ System:** Alimentary / oral soft tissues

**Secondary Organ System:** Lymphatic (regional lymph nodes); skin

**Transmission:** Entry via injuries to oral mucosa or skin (coarse feed, teeth eruptions, thorns, tick bites); commensal of oral mucosa and oropharynx

**Clinical Signs:**

- Weight loss, emaciation

- Hard circumscribed nodules on skin/soft tissues of mouth, tongue and face → become soft, fluctuate and discharge contents leaving ulcers or fistulae

- Pus: thick, mucoid, greenish-yellow to bright yellow containing sulphur granules (Splendore-Hoepli bodies)

- Abscess or granuloma of teats and udders of sows (occasional)

**Pathological signs:**

- Skin and soft tissues of mouth, tongue: Pyogranulomatous inflammation

- Regional lymph nodes: Affected with granulomas and pus containing sulphur granules

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Commensal on oral mucosa → entry via mucosal injury → pyogranulomatous inflammation of skin and soft tissues → nodule formation → abscess/fistula → spread to regional lymph nodes

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Site of lesions, type of exudate with sulphur granules when mixed with water

- Culture of exudate and Splendore-Hoepli bodies

- Microscopic exam of washed sulphur granules
