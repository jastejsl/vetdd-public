**Common names:** Bovine paratyphoid; Bovine salmonellosis

**Affects****:** Cattle; Calves under 3 months

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal

**Secondary Organ System:** Cardiovascular; Reproductive; Respiratory

**Transmission:** Faecal-oral from clinically ill or carrier animals

**Clinical Signs:**

- Calves: Enteritis — high fever, putty-like to brown malodorous/watery stool, dehydration; death after 5–7 days in untreated calves (mortality 60%)

- Adults (rare): Fever, anorexia, malaise, decreased milk production, abortion

- Chronic: Emaciation, dehydration, abdominal pain

- Systemic infection: Septicaemia, pneumonia, polyarthritis, osteitis

Peracute:

- Calves: Typical septicaemia pathology

**Pathological signs:**

- Dehydration organs commonly affected: liver, lung, spleen, GIT

- Liver: Bronze with many small grey-white necrotic foci; 

- GIT: Necrotizing or diphtheritic jejunitis and ileitis

- Chronic: Fibrinopurulent synovitis, bronchopneumonia, abscessation in lungs, meningoencephalitis, osteomyelitis, skin necrosis of tips of ears, tail, limbs

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Per os → survival and proliferation in GIT → colonization and invasion of intestinal mucosa → endotoxin production → inflammation and secretion of large amounts of fluid and electrolytes → cellular infiltration and damage to villi → malabsorption and maldigestion → diarrhoea (can disseminate systemically via regional lymph nodes) → septicaemia

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology
  - Characteristic hepatic lesions: bronze liver with grey-white necrotic foci

- Culture from faeces intestines, mesenteric lymph nodes  (multiple if clinical disease)
  - Gall bladder ( if PM)

- Septicaemia: fresh organs

**Zoonosis:** yes
