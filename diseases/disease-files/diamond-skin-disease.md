**Caused by:** Erysipelothrix rhusiopathiae

**Common names:** Swine erysipelas; Erysipelas

**Affects:** Pigs (primary), mammals, birds, fish

Four clinical forms: peracute/acute septicaemia, subacute vasculitis (diamond skin disease), chronic polyarthritis, chronic vegetative valvular endocarditis

**Primary Organ System:** Cardiovascular (septicaemia; valvular endocarditis)

**Secondary Organ System:** Integumentary (diamond skin); Musculoskeletal (polyarthritis)

**Transmission:** Ingestion of contaminated food, soil, faeces or through wounds/biting flies; carrier pigs (tonsils and lymph tissues); environment; birds and rodents (minor)

**Clinical Signs:**

Peracute:

- Sudden death; pyrexia (\>41°C); conjunctivitis; purple blotches on skin; hindlimb weakness and stiff gait; haemorrhagic diarrhoea; 50–100% mortality

Acute:

- Subacute vasculitis (diamond skin disease): fever, abortion (sows); rhomboid/diamond-shaped patches on skin (light to purple, very hyperaemic, slightly raised); severe cases: necrotic, slough, ulcers

- Chronic non-suppurative polyarthritis: arched back, walk on toes, unthrifty; synovitis; most common form in SA

- Chronic vegetative valvular endocarditis: mitral and aortic valves most commonly affected; congestive heart failure — death

**Pathological signs:**

- Skin: Rhomboid/diamond-shaped necrotic lesions (subacute form)

- Heart valves: Vegetative valvular endocarditis (chronic form)

- Joints: Chronic non-suppurative polyarthritis; synovitis

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Ingestion → infection via tonsils and gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) → organism survives and multiplies in macrophages of non-immune pigs → neuraminidase allows bacteria to attach to and invade endothelial cells → capsule protects bacterium from phagocytosis;

- Subacute form: arteritis and thrombosis of arterioles in dermis → sharply circumscribed cutaneous diamond-shaped lesions

- Chronic form: immune complexes (Type III hypersensitivity) + complement and neutrophil activation → lysosomal enzymes damage cartilage and other tissues in joints → polyarthritis; localization of bacteria on heart valves → chronic valvular endocarditis → valvular insufficiency → chronic congestive heart failure

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Culture and ID from blood and internal organs (septicaemia) or dermis (subacute)

- PCR

- Serology: herd test for chronic infection (not vaccinated herds)

**Zoonosis:** yes

Minor zoonosis; well controlled in SA with vaccination
