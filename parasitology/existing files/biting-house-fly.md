**Latin name:** Stomoxys calcitrans

**Other names:** Stable fly

**Affects:** Domestic livestock; horses; cattle; dogs; humans

Biting cyclorrhaphan fly; proboscis rigid and projects forward; both males and females take blood meals during daylight only; painful bites on ears of dogs and pigs, backline of lambs, cattle and horses, and lower legs of humans.

**Primary Organ System:** Integument

**Predilection site:** Ears of dogs and pigs; backline of lambs, cattle and horses; lower legs of humans

**Identification tips:** Rigid forward-projecting proboscis; wings held slightly divergent; M1 and M2 veins NOT markedly bent; head up at rest; only daytime biting; larvae with S-shaped spiracular openings with 3 openings on each peritreme

**Carrier of:** *Habronema majus* (Intermediate host)

**Clinical Signs:**

- Presence

- Painful bites causing biting stress

- Fly worry; irritation

- Habronemiasis via *Habronema majus*: gastric nodules in horses; cutaneous habronemiasis

**Pathological signs:**

- GIT: gastric nodules (*Habronema majus*)

- Integument: cutaneous habronemiasis at wound sites

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- *Habronema majus* larvae develop in *Stomoxys* maggots → deposited by fly on horse wound or mucous membrane → larvae cause gastric and cutaneous habronemiasis; direct biting causes pain and blood loss

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Identification of stable fly in environment

- Biopsy of skin lesions: *Habronema majus* larvae
