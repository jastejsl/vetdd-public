**Caused by:** Dermatophilus congolensis

**Affects:** Cattle, sheep, horses, goats, dogs, cats, humans (wide host range); common in tropical regions; young animals particularly affected

**Primary Organ System:** Integumentary (skin/epidermis)

**Transmission:** Direct contact between infected animals; indirect contact via contaminated environment or dipping; biting insects; skin injuries; wet skin and lesions predispose to infection; rain, high humidity and temperature

**Clinical Signs:**

- Scabs or crusts

- Matted hair or wool

- Raw areas where crusts detach

- Secondary infections common

- Progression: matted fur → crust/scab → proliferation of keratinized material (wart-like)

**Pathological signs:**

- Skin/epidermis: Exudative dermatitis with scab formation

- Epidermis: Thick laminated crust composed of alternating layers of orthokeratosis, hyperkeratosis and exudate

- Skin: Acute inflammation with neutrophil exudate

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Moisture stimulates dormant zoospores to lose outer capsule and become motile (flagellum) → positive chemotaxis toward CO2 diffusing through skin → zoospores breach skin barrier → germinate and produce hyphae → hyphae invade living cell layers of epidermis → extensive proliferation stimulating keratinization and acute inflammation (neutrophil exudate) → hyphae divide longitudinally and transversely producing cocci → cocci mature into more zoospores → new epidermis invaded from the side → thick laminated crust (scab) → lesions heal spontaneously in 1–2 weeks

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Cytology: fresh crusts, impression smears showing 'railroad track' pattern

- Bacterial culture

- IFA, ELISA

**Zoonosis:** yes
