**Caused by:** L. Pomona, L. Tarassovi, L. Bratislava, L. Canicola, L. Hardjo

**Affects:** All mammals; swine (most important in SA), dogs, cattle, rodents, wildlife

Worldwide zoonotic disease; most widespread zoonosis in nature; important cause of abortions, stillbirths and reproductive failure in cattle and pigs

**Primary Organ System:** Reproductive

**Secondary Organ System:** Urinary; Hepatic; Ophthalmic

**Transmission:** Urine from maintenance hosts (shed in high numbers); semen (AI); drinking water; pens; aerosols; transplacental and venereal transmission; incidental hosts infected indirectly through damp environments; maintenance hosts shed bacteria in urine, placenta fluid and milk

**Clinical Signs:**

- Fever and joint/muscle pain

- Vomiting and diarrhoea

- Haemolytic anaemia

- Bleeding tendency

- Jaundice

- Frequent urination (may progress to lack of urination — kidney failure)

- Reproductive failures (abortions, stillbirths)

- Swine: reproductive failure, infertility, abortion

- Dogs: liver and kidney compromise

- Cattle: reproductive losses (abortion and stillbirths)

Acute:

- Rare: acute haemolytic crisis and liver disease (mainly dogs)

- Subacute: reproductive wastage (abortions); L. pomona: last trimester; L. hardjo: throughout gestation (can permanently affect fertility)

Chronic:

- Chronic interstitial nephritis (CIN), fever, joint/muscle pain, decreased appetite, weakness, vomiting with blood, diarrhoea, discharge from nose and eyes, frequent urination, jaundice

**Pathological signs:**

- Kidneys: Chronic lesions — scattered grey foci surrounded by zone of inflammation (chronic interstitial nephritis)

- Liver: Focal necrosis; jaundice in foetuses

- Organisms localize in proximal renal tubules; shed in high numbers for \~1 month

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Entry via mucous membranes and skin → bloodstream → spreads to liver, pregnant uterus, kidneys → adhesion to cell surfaces; cytotoxic sphingomyelinase → hepatocellular damage, haemorrhages in lungs/mucous membranes/kidneys; haemolysin (phospholipase) → destruction of cell membranes → haemolytic anaemia, agalactiae; LPS → fever, coagulation defects; autoimmunity in previously exposed animals → Moon blindness in horses, thrombocytopaenia; localization in pregnant uterus → abortion, stillbirths

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Serological MAT tests using live leptospiral organisms (dam, foetal blood)

- Dark field microscopy

- Culture (viability and time required = difficult; not recommended routinely)

- IFAT

- PCR

**Zoonosis:** yes

---

About the specific causative agents:

- L. Pomona & L. Tarassovi: abortions, stillbirths, chronic interstitial nephritis in pigs

- L. Bratislava: venereal infection causing embryonal death and infertility in pigs

- L. Canicola: abortions, stillbirths, chronic interstitial nephritis in dogs

- L. Hardjo: abortions, stillbirths, chronic interstitial nephritis in cattle
