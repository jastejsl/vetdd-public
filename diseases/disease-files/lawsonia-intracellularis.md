**Affects:** Pigs (6–20 weeks), horses (weanling foals 4–7 months), hamsters, rabbits, fox, deer, ferrets, ostriches, primates

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal (small and large intestine)

**Transmission:** Faecal-oral route; bacteria shed in faeces; animals can develop latent infections and remain carriers

**Clinical Signs:**

- Anorexia

- Diarrhoea

- Stunted growth

- Acute death

- Weight loss

- Pigs (porcine proliferative enteritis): variable clinical signs; acute death, diarrhoea, weight loss or no clinical signs

- Equine proliferative enteritis (foals): decreased appetite, depression, weight loss, diarrhoea, fever, oedema, poor body condition

**Pathological signs:**

- Intestinal tract: Dilated and swollen/turgid intestines with corrugated folding; mucosa hypertrophied with deep transverse folds, with/without haemorrhage

- Necrotic membranes

- Intestinal crypts: Hyperplasia of crypt enterocytes (adenoma-like mucosa); hypertrophic intestinal crypts

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Bacteria invade and multiply in enterocytes and macrophages of small and large intestine → prevent maturation of enterocytes and stimulate their proliferation → immature epithelial cells of intestinal crypts become hyperplastic → adenoma-like mucosa → diarrhoea and weight loss

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Histology of intestinal mucosa for proliferative lesions

- Demonstration of bacteria in tissue sections

- Immunofluorescence assays (IFA) of faecal smears

- PCR assays on ileal scrapings/faecal samples

- Immunoperoxidase staining of ileal scrapings/faecal smears

- Immunohistochemistry

- Serology (ELISA)
