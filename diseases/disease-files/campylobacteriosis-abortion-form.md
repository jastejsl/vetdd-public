**Common names:** Bovine genital campylobacteriosis (BGC)

**Caused by:** Campylobacter fetus subsp. fetus

**Affects:** Cattle (primarily); sheep

**Primary Organ System:** Reproductive

**Secondary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal (reservoir)

**Transmission:** Venereal; can survive many months in frozen semen; found in prepuce of carrier bulls

**Clinical Signs:**

- Sporadic abortions (mid to late gestation)

- Great variation in gestation lengths

- Cows: infertility, metritis (mucopurulent endometritis), prolonged luteal phases, irregular oestrous cycles, vaginitis

- Bulls: transient infection; no clinical signs; semen quality unaffected

**Pathological signs:**

- Placenta: Foetuses: placenta, liver, lungs, stomach content affected

- Endometrium: Subacute to chronic endometritis

- Oviducts: Salpingitis (in persistent infection — permanent sterility in 10% of heifers)

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Organisms in intestinal tract → venereal transmission → localize in penis and prepuce → coitus → migrate from vagina to uterus to oviducts (within 7 days) → subacute to chronic endometritis + decreased oxygen tension → embryonic death → prolonged oestrus cycle (\~40 days) → sporadic abortion

**Diagnosis & Samples:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Isolation from genital tract or internal organs of aborted foetuses (placenta, liver, lungs, stomach content)
  - Male: preputial smegma and semen
  - Female: cervico-vaginal mucus (CVM)
  - Vaginal mucus agglutination test (VMAT)

- Cultured at 37°C in microaerobic environment

Deliver samples to laboratory within 6 hours (or use transport medium)

Tests that do not work well:

- ELISA (cannot differentiate between subspecies)

- IFAT (cannot differentiate between different subspecies)

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes
