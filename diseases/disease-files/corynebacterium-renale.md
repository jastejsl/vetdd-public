**Common names:** Contagious bovine pyelonephritis

**Affects:** Cattle (primarily), sheep

Most common cause of urinary tract infections in the renale group; often seen after parturition

**Primary Organ System:** Urinary

**Secondary Organ System:** Reproductive (transmission facilitated by parturition)

**Transmission:** Urine aerosol spray onto vulva of susceptible cows in close contact; venereal transmission; careless use of AI catheters

**Clinical Signs:**

- First indication: blood-stained urine in an otherwise normal cow

- Frequent attempts to urinate, anorexia, loss of production, colic, polyuria, haematuria or pyuria

- Decreased milk production

- Loss of condition, tense abdominal wall

Chronic:

- Colic, diarrhoea, polyuria, polydipsia, stranguria, and anaemia

**Pathological signs:**

- Bladder: Thickened and inflamed

- Ureters: Thickened and dilated with purulent exudate

- Kidneys: Multiple small abscesses on surface extending into cortex and medulla

**Pathophysiology:**

- Bacteria part of normal flora of vagina/bulls → urine aerosol or coitus → spread to vaginal mucosa (grow in increased urea and elevated pH) → ascending infection to bladder, ureters, renal pelvis → pyelonephritis

- Predisposing factors: trauma to bladder/ureters during parturition; high protein diet increasing urine pH; multiparous cows

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Haematuria, recent parturition history, palpation of left kidney for enlargement and pain

- Haematology

- Endoscopic vaginal inspection: inflamed and enlarged ureters

- Urinalysis
  - Collect midstream urine (bloody, cloudy, offensive odour)

**Lab Findings**:

- Left-shift neutrophilia

- Increased blood creatinine and urea, decreased sodium

- Urine culture reveals C. renale group along with Streptococcus agalactiae

- Urine: proteinuria and haematuria on dipstick
