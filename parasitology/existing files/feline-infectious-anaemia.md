**Caused by:** Mycoplasma haemofelis 

**Affects:** Cats (mainly); dogs (rare)

Acute or chronic disease; life cycle unknown except that arthropods are vectors; transmitted by biting arthropods.

**Primary Organ System:** Haematological

**Predilection site:** Surface of erythrocytes

**Identification tips:** Attaches to RBC surface; activated to proliferate during stress; arthropod vectors; includes genera Haemobartonella + Eperythrozoon.

**Clinical Signs:**

- Anaemia

- Depression

- Icterus

- Fever (only during acute stage)

- Anorexia

- Splenomegaly

**Pathological signs:**

- Blood: Severe anaemia; splenomegaly; RBC hyperplasia (reddened bone marrow); mild lymphoid tissue hyperplasia

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Parasite attaches to RBC surface → activated to proliferate during stress → increased erythrophagocytosis in spleen and bone marrow → after 2+ parasitaemia episodes: anaemia pronounced due to substantial RBC phagocytosis

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Blood smear (signs of regenerative anaemia); PCV; erythrocyte counts

**Note:** Is considered a “parasite” but classified as a bacterium hence text above focuses on it as a parasite. Will await feedback for clarification if needed.
