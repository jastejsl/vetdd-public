**Affects:** Cattle (beef feedlot, dairy calves); sheep and goats 

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory

**Secondary Organ System:** Mammary (mastitis — blue bag in sheep); Cardiovascular (septicaemia)

**Transmission:** Commensal of URT; spread by aerosol; stress-induced proliferation; transport, dehorning, weaning, viral infection predispose; feedlot cattle 45 days of introduction most affected

**Clinical Signs:**

- Depression, fever

- Serous to mucopurulent nasal discharge

- Moist cough

- Shallow respiratory rate

- Auscultation of cranioventral lung: increased bronchial sounds, crackles, wheezing, grunting

- Preceded by signs of viral infection of respiratory tract; onset of bacterial pneumonia: increased severity, depression, toxaemia

- Neonates (shipping fever form): coughing fits, extended head and neck, laterally rotated elbows, breathing through open mouth, projecting tongue

Acute:

- Pneumonia (cranioventral lobes); high temperature; nasal discharge; high mortality; rapid breathing; cyanosis (skin and ear tips)

**Pathological signs:**

- Lungs: Severe, acute fibrinonecrotic pneumonia; extensive reddish-black/greyish-brown regions; thickening of interlobular septa; fibrinous pleuritis; extensive thromboses and foci of lung necrosis

- Serosal surfaces: Severe generalized congestion; widespread petechiae

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Normal inhabitant of nasopharynx → stress factors (shipping, viral infection) reduce immunity → proliferation → mucociliary escalator paralysed → bacteria grow down into lungs → severe acute fibrinonecrotic pneumonia → respiratory failure

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Gross necropsy findings (lung samples)

- Bacterial culture from pleural fluid, blood, nasal excretion, pericardial exudates, lungs

- PCR (most accurate)

- Serology
