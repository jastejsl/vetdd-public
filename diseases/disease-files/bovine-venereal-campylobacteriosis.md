**Latin name:** Campylobacter fetus subsp. venerealis

**Affects:** Cattle

One of the two most important causes of infertility and poor calving rates in South Africa; endemic pattern

**Primary Organ System:** Reproductive

**Transmission:** Venereal (sexually transmitted from males to females via bacterial shed in semen); bulls older than 3 years can become persistently infected and act as reservoir hosts

**Clinical Signs:**

- Infertility

- Metritis: mucopurulent endometritis

- Prolonged luteal phases

- Irregular oestrus cycles, repeat breeding

- Vaginitis

- Abortion (mid to late gestation)

- Great variation in gestation lengths

- Bulls: no clinical signs; semen quality and breeding ability unaffected

**Pathological signs:**

- Endometrium: Inflammation and lowered O2 tension creating unfavourable environment for embryonic survival

- Oviducts: Bilateral salpingitis leading to permanent sterility

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Organisms located in penis and prepuce epithelial surface → coitus → migrate from vagina to uterus to oviducts (25%, within 7 days) → subacute to chronic endometritis + decreased O2 tension → embryonic death → prolonged oestrus cycle (\~40 days) → self-limiting (recovery 3 months–2 years) OR persistent infection in cervix → infertility + irregular inter-oestrus intervals; salpingitis → permanent sterility in 10% of heifers

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Isolation from preputial sheath washing with 20–30 ml sterile PBS (recollected and transported within 6 hours)

- Female: cervico-vaginal mucus (CVM)

- Vaginal mucus agglutination test (VMAT) — 10% of herd

- ELISA to test herds

- IFAT

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes
