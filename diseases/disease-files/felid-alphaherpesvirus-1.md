**Specific Causes:** Felid alphaherpesvirus 1 (FHV-1)

**Affects:** Domestic and wild felids only

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory; Ocular

**Secondary Organ System:** Oral

**Transmission:** Shed in oculo-nasal discharges and saliva (in smaller amounts in urine/faeces); contact with mucous membranes including conjunctiva; stress reactivates latent carriers

**Clinical Signs:**

- Sneezing

- Oculo-nasal discharge (serous to mucopurulent)

- Pyrexia

- Oral ulcerations

- Conjunctivitis

- Open-mouthed breathing; anorexia; dehydration

- Chronic complications: rhinitis, sinusitis

**Pathological signs:**

- Ocular: multifocal necrosis of conjunctival and corneal epithelium

- Respiratory: necrosis of upper respiratory tract epithelium

- Oral: ulceration

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- After 2–6-day incubation, replication in superficial epithelium of conjunctiva and upper respiratory tract → multifocal necrosis → spreads along neurons to trigeminal ganglia → latent state; stress → reactivation → episodic viral shedding

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- PCR; viral isolation; antigen detection
