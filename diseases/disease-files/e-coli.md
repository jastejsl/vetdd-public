**Full name:** Escherichia coli

**Affects:** Pigs, cattle, sheep, goats, horses; sows; young animals and neonates most severely affected

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal

**Secondary Organ System:** Cardiovascular; Urinary; Respiratory; Nervous system

**Transmission:** Faecal-oral (diarrhoea); colisepticaemia may also be via umbilicus; ingestion of very large numbers of pathogenic E. coli required for colonization; carrier dams and persistently infected environment

**Clinical Signs:**

- Enteritis: Diarrhoea, dehydration (white scours in calves)

- Urinary tract infection: frequent urination, discomfort

- Pneumonia: fever, cough

- Mastitis: inflamed udder, fever, collapse

Peracute:

- Colisepticaemia: listlessness, depression, cold extremities, weak pulse, tachycardia, collapse, recumbency; mortality close to 100%

Acute:

- Colisepticaemia (more prolonged, \~1 week): localization (polyarthritis, ophthalmitis, omphalophlebitis, meningitis)

- Oedema disease (pigs): sudden death; subcutaneous oedema (head, eyelids, ears, nose); oedema of stomach and colon; brain damage (staggering gait, incoordination, paralysis, muscle twitching); laryngeal oedema (squeaky voice)

**Pathological signs:**

- Intestinal tract: Fluid-filled, congested and oedematous SI; mesenteric lymph nodes enlarged; hindquarters soiled with faeces

- Serosal surfaces: Generalized congestion, petechiae and ecchymoses

- Meninges: Congestion; fibrinopurulent lesions in less acute cases

- Brain/vessels: Shiga toxin damages small blood vessels causing widespread subcutaneous oedema (oedema disease)

**Pathophysiology:**

- ETEC (calves <2 weeks): Bacteria attach by pili to enterocytes → toxin production → biochemical changes in enterocytes → hypersecretion of fluids and electrolytes (Na, Cl + reduced absorption) into intestinal lumen → osmotic diarrhoea

- AEEC (calves <4 months): Adheres to enterocyte surface in caecum and colon → transfers proteins into host cell → cytoskeletal rearrangements → secretion of fluid from cell → attaching and effacing lesions → haemorrhagic/mucoid diarrhoea

- Colisepticaemia: Per os or via umbilicus → usually invasive → extracts iron from serum → endotoxin-induced inflammatory response → endotoxic shock; survivors may develop fibrinopurulent meningitis or arthritis

- Oedema disease (STEC, pigs 1–4 weeks post-weaning): Shiga toxin E. coli binds to F18 receptors on microvilli → produces Shiga toxin → damages small blood vessels (including brain vessels → nervous signs) → 50% mortality; subcutaneous oedema, stomach/mesenterium oedema

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Age group, production system, white scours with severe dehydration and acidosis, necropsy (catarrhal enteritis)

- Faeces/rectal swabs of at least 10 calves (untreated); ileum (ETEC); ileum and colon (AEEC 2–3 sections)

- Culture and identification; toxin ID using biotechnology

**Zoonosis:** yes
