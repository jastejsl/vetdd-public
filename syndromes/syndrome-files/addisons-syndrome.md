**Affects:** Dog ; Cat (rare)

**Primary Organ System:** Endocrine

**Secondary Organ System:** Cardiovascular ; Renal ; GIT ; Neuromuscular

**Predisposition:** Young to middle-aged female dogs; Great Danes, Poodles, West Highland White Terriers, Bearded Collies

Concurrent immune-mediated diseases: autoimmune anaemia, hepatitis, hypothyroidism, thrombocytopaenia.

**Clinical Signs:**

- Anorexia and weight loss

- Lethargy and depression

- Vomiting; diarrhoea; abdominal pain

- Muscle weakness

- Polyuria and polydipsia (Na washout → dilute urine)

- Shaking

- Bradycardia (hyperkalaemia-induced)

**Addisonian Crisis (acute, life-threatening):**

- Collapse and circulatory shock (hypovolaemia + loss of vascular α₁ receptor sensitivity)

- Severe dehydration

- Bradyarrhythmias (hyperkalaemia — ECG: tenting of T wave, widened QRS complexes, disappearance of P waves)

- Severe hypoglycaemia (cortisol is the principal driver of gluconeogenesis)

- Metabolic acidosis

**Pathological signs:**

- Adrenal cortex: bilateral atrophy/destruction of all 3 zones (typical Addisons) OR inner 2 zones only with Z. glomerulosa sparing (atypical Addisons)

- Microcardia (occasionally — hypovolaemia)

- Megaoesophagus (attributed to electrolyte imbalance + cortisol deficiency; resolves with treatment)

- Lymph nodes/spleen: lymphocytosis and eosinophilia (absence of cortisol-induced suppression)

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism of development:**

1. Typical Addisons:

Immune-mediated  / granulomatous inflammation / DIC / metastatic neoplasia / iatrogenic  | (initial insult/origin)

→ progressive destruction of all 3 zones of adrenal cortex (>90% tissue must be lost before CS appear)

→ loss of both aldosterone (Z. glomerulosa) AND cortisol (Z. fasciculata)

→ aldosterone deficiency: Na ⁺ / Cl ⁻  loss → osmotic diuresis → dehydration

→ pre-renal azotaemia + K ⁺  retention

→ hyperkalaemia

→ cardiotoxic bradyarrhythmias; cortisol deficiency: loss of gluconeogenesis

→ hypoglycaemia; loss of vascular tone

→ hypotension; loss of stress response

→ Addisonian crisis on any major stressor

2. Atypical Addisons:

Destruction of inner layers only (Z. fasciculata + Z. reticularis) with Z. glomerulosa preserved

→ isolated glucocorticoid deficiency

→ normal Na:K ratio; vague GIT signs; hypoglycaemia; normal electrolytes

3. Secondary Addisons

Hypothalamic/pituitary lesion → ↓ACTH → cortisol deficiency only

Aldosterone unaffected — controlled by RAAS, not ACTH under normal conditions)

OR

Sudden withdrawal of chronic glucocorticoid therapy → adrenal atrophy → transient cortisol deficiency

**Diagnosis:**

- ACTH stimulation test (TEST OF CHOICE): basal and post-ACTH cortisol both low/flat — confirms hypoadrenocorticism; plasma aldosterone can also be measured

- Endogenous ACTH: elevated in primary Addisons; low/normal in secondary Addisons

- ECG: bradycardia, tenting of T wave, shortened QT interval, widened QRS, disappearance of P waves — correlate with K ⁺  level

**Lab findings:**

- Serum electrolytes:

- Hyponatraemia

- Hypochloraemia

- Hyperkalaemia

- Na:K ratio <23:1 (normal 27:1–40:1) consistent with Addisons (NOT pathognomonic)

- Serum biochemistry:

- Pre-renal azotaemia (urea + creatinine elevated)

- Hypoglycaemia (~25%)

- Hypoalbuminaemia

- Hypocholesterolaemia

- Elevated liver enzymes

- Hypercalcaemia (decreased renal Ca excretion due to hypocortisolaemia)

- Haematology:

- Mild normocytic normochromic non-regenerative anaemia

- Eosinophilia and lymphocytosis (tip-off sign in a sick/stressed dog that should have eosinopaenia)

- Urinalysis: low SG (dilute urine; Na washout)
