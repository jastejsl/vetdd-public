**Caused by:** Mycoplasma mycoides subsp. mycoides

**Affects:** Water buffalo and cattle only (wild bovids appear resistant)

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory

**Secondary Organ System:** Joints (arthritis in young animals — calves up to 6 months)

**Transmission:** 

- Direct contact (droplets emitted by coughing, saliva and urine) with diseased cattle or subclinical carriers; 

- Aerosol

- Transplacental infection can occur

- Chronic cases with encapsulated lesions can break down and act as a source of infection

**Clinical Signs:**

- Incubation period 3–6 weeks

Acute:

- Fever

- Loss of appetite and depression

- Rapid abdominal respiration, grunt at expiration

- Epistaxis, purulent or mucoid nasal discharges, coughing and chest pain

- Abortions and stillbirths

- Recovered animals are weak and emaciated

- Calves up to 6 months may only show arthritis (mycoplasma cannot escape from joints)

Subacute and chronic:

- Clinical signs milder

- Rise in temperature

- Loss of condition

- Co-existence of pulmonary signs in adults with arthritis in young animals is a key diagnostic feature

**Pathological signs:**

Acute:

- Fibrinous pleuropneumonia

- Severe pneumonia with lots of yellow exudate

- One or both lungs may be completely consolidated with a characteristic "marbled" appearance

- Affected areas pink to dark red, swollen and firm

- Usually unilateral lesions

Chronic:

- Necrotic lung tissue becomes encapsulated (sequestra)

- Pleural adhesions common

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Organism attaches to ciliated epithelium in lower respiratory tract → acute fibrinous pleuropneumonia → in chronic disease, necrotic lung tissue becomes encapsulated as sequestra, which can later break down to act as a source of infection

**Diagnosis:**

- Serological tests: Complement Fixation test, ELISA, Latex Agglutination and Immunoblotting tests

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes (Controlled disease)

**Note: M**assive economic losses (highly contagious, comparable to FMDV), reduced meat production, up to 90% drop in milk production, reduced performance of draught animals
