**Specific Causes:** Feline calicivirus

**Affects:** Domestic and wild felids

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory; Oral

**Secondary Organ System:** Ocular

**Transmission:** Close contact; oculonasal discharge; saliva; fomites (infective 8–10 days); carriers shed continuously

**Clinical Signs:**

- Sneezing

- Conjunctival signs

- Pyrexia

- Serous/mucopurulent nasal and ocular discharge

- Oral ulcerations (most prominent pathological feature)

- Hypersalivation

- Open-mouthed breathing

- Anorexia; dehydration

- Pneumonia (sometimes secondary bacterial)

- Chronic complications: rhinitis, sinusitis

**Pathological signs:**

- Oral: ulceration of oral epithelium

- Respiratory: serous/mucoid rhinitis; sub-acute interstitial pneumonia

- Ocular: conjunctivitis

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Replication in oropharynx during 2–10-day incubation → necrosis of endothelial cells → vesicles on tongue rapidly become ulcers; pneumonia sometimes encountered

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- PCR; viral isolation; antigen detection
