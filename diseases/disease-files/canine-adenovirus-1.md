**Specific Causes:** Canine mastadenovirus A (CAV-1)

**Affects:** Dogs; foxes; walrus; sea lion

**Primary Organ System:** Hepatic; Renal

**Secondary Organ System:** Vascular endothelium; Ocular; Coagulation

**Tranmission:** Oronasal routes; indirect contact via fomites; virus excreted in all body fluids (especially urine and faeces for 6–9 months)

**Clinical Signs:**

- Listlessness; abdominal pain

- Polydipsia

- Congestion of mucous membranes

- Red, inflamed tonsils

- Lymphadenitis

- Vomiting and abdominal pain

- Interstitial keratitis (blue eye) in 25% of cases

- Blood-stained diarrhoea

- Petechial haemorrhages

Acute:

- Icterus (jaundice)

- DIC

**Pathological signs:**

- Hepatic: necrosis of endothelial cells; liver swollen, yellow, mottled; centrilobular necrosis; fibrin strands on liver

- Vascular: haemorrhages in walls of stomach, intestines, heart

- Renal: immune-complex glomerulonephritis; focal interstitial nephritis

- Ocular: interstitial keratitis (blue eye)

- CNS: brain lesions in midbrain and thalamus; viral inclusions in thalamus

- Gall bladder: haemorrhage and oedema

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Virus localises in tonsils and regional lymphoid tissue → spreads via lymphatics to blood → infects hepatic parenchymal cells and vascular endothelial cells and kidneys → centrilobular liver necrosis + immune-complex glomerulonephritis + DIC + keratitis

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Elevated liver enzymes (ALT disproportionately high vs. bilirubin)

- Haematology and clotting profile (leukopenia)

- Serology (seroconversion)

- Virus isolation

- Histopathology: hepatic lesions; viral inclusions in hepatocytes and glomerular endothelial cells
