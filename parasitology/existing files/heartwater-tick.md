**Latin name:** Amblyomma hebraeum

**Common names:** Heartwater tick; bont tick

**Affects:** Cattle; sheep; goats

3-host tick

- Larvae on feet, legs and muzzle

- Nymphs on feet, legs, groin, sternum and neck

- Adults on hairless regions under tail, perineum, udder and teats, prepuce and axilla

**Primary Organ System:** Cardiovascular / CNS (via *Ehrlichia ruminantium*)

**Secondary Organ System:** Integument

**Predilection site:** 3-host life cycle shared above, feet of sheep and goats

**Identification tips:** 3-host ornate bont tick; colourful pattern on scutum; adults on perineum and udder; long mouthparts causing abscesses; major vector of *Ehrlichia ruminantium* (Heartwater)

**Carrier of:** *Ehrlichia ruminantium* (Heartwater)

**Clinical Signs:**

- Clinical signs primarily from transmitted *Ehrlichia ruminantium* (Heartwater)
  - High fever
  - Nervous signs (circling, high-stepping, chewing movements, convulsions)
  - Hydropericardium and hydrothorax
  - Sudden death

- Secondary: abscesses and screwworm infestation at mouthpart attachment wounds

**Pathological signs:**

- Due to heartwater
  - Cardiovascular: hydropericardium
  - Thoracic cavity: hydrothorax, pulmonary oedema
  - Integument: abscesses; *Cochliomyia bezziana* infestation at attachment wounds

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Tick feeds → inoculates *E. ruminantium* → infects vascular endothelial cells → vasculitis → increased vascular permeability → hydropericardium, pulmonary oedema, cerebral oedema → neurological signs and death; mouthparts cause deep skin wounds → abscesses and screwworm infestation

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes

**Diagnosis:**

- Pathology

- Identification of bont tick

- Check concurrent disease diagnostics

**Lab Findings**:

- Brain crush smear: *E. ruminantium* colonies in endothelial cells
