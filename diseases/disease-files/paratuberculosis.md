**Affects:** Sheep and goats; cattle

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal 

**Secondary Organ System:** Lymphatic

**Transmission:** Mostly faecal-oral route; in utero; lambs via teats soiled with infected faeces; ingestion of milk from infected dams; the organism survives months (up to 1 year) in moist soils

**Clinical Signs:**

Sheep and goats:

- Afebrile

- Chronic wasting (extreme emaciation)

- Faeces soft and pasty with soiling of perineum

- Shedding of wool may occur in some sheep

- Appetite depressed in severely affected sheep

- Subcutaneous oedema of submandibular region is rare

Cattle:

- Clinical signs usually only appear after the age of 4 years

- Muscle wasting and low milk yield

- Pipe stream diarrhoea

- Bottle jaw

- Symptoms become gradually worse, leading to malnutrition, debilitation and death

**Pathological signs:**

- GIT: thickened small intestine; thickened lymphatics; erosions of small intestine; thickening and corrugation of mucosa of small intestine; lesions typically in terminal ileum and ileocaecal-colic lymph nodes

- Histopathology: numerous organisms found in clumps (nests) in epithelioid cells of intestine and mesenteric lymph nodes

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Incubation period 15–18 months (very long) → ingested and phagocytosed by macrophages and monocytes (M-cells) of Peyer's patches of ileal mucosa → multiplies slowly in macrophages (intracellular) in sub-mucosa → replication stimulates granulomatous inflammation in ileum and regional lymphatics/lymph nodes → lesions develop a few months after infection and later involve the whole intestine and regional lymph nodes → thickening of intestinal mucosa and mesenteric lymph node enlargement → increased leakage of plasma proteins and mal-absorption of amino acids → hypoproteinaemia → rarely bacilli carried by macrophages to other organs (liver, spleen, uterus, udder, male reproductive tracts)

**Diagnosis****:**

- Ante-mortem: direct pathogen detection 
  - acid-fast small rods (nests) via faecal smear exam, faecal culture & PCR
  - serology (ELISA) — not very sensitive, used to confirm test

- Post-mortem: typical lesions; 
  - Microscopy of rectal scrapings or ileal impression smears;
  - Histopathology with demonstration of characteristic lesions and numerous acid-fast organisms
  - Culture and identification of organisms is confirmatory
    - Sample sites: Rectal mucosa scrapings, terminal ileum, mesenteric lymph nodes, ileo-caecal valve area
  - Histopathology combined with PCR of tissue sections is fastest and most reliable

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes

**Zoonosis:** Possible link to Crohn's disease in humans

**Notes:**

- Economic impact: wool break, reduced milk production, poor weight gain, emaciation, deaths

- Vaccination decreases shedding and clinical disease but does NOT prevent infection, and interferes with immunological testing

- Slaughter of repeatedly positive animals is conducted from a management and economic lens.
