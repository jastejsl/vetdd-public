**Common names:** Midge; Biting midge

**Affects:** Sheep (Bluetongue virus); horses (African Horse Sickness); cattle

Smallest blood-sucking fly of veterinary importance; nocturnal feeder; cannot bite through clothing; found in frost-free areas and warm summer rainfall regions.

**Primary Organ System:** Integument / haematopoietic

**Secondary Organ System:** Respiratory; musculoskeletal

**Predilection site:** Exposed skin; cannot penetrate clothing

**Identification tips:** Smallest blood-sucking fly; humped thorax with humeral pits; mottled wings held in scissor-like fashion at rest; long many-segmented antennae; nocturnal; swarms

**Carrier of:** Bluetongue virus (sheep); African Horse Sickness (AHS) virus (horses)

**Clinical Signs:**

- Biting stress and irritation from swarms

- Clinical signs primarily from transmitted Bluetongue (sheep) and AHS (horses)

- Bluetongue: fever, facial oedema, tongue cyanosis, coronitis, lameness

- AHS: respiratory distress, oedema, high mortality in horses

**Pathological signs:**

- Integument: bite wounds; oedema and cyanosis of tongue and lips (Bluetongue)

- Respiratory: pulmonary oedema (AHS)

- Musculoskeletal: coronitis; lameness

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Nocturnal feeding → ingests/inoculates Bluetongue virus or AHS virus → viraemia in host → endothelial damage → oedema, haemorrhage, necrosis of tongue, coronary band and muscles

**Diagnosis:**

- Identification of midge vector in area

- Virus isolation or PCR for Bluetongue / AHS virus

- Pathology
