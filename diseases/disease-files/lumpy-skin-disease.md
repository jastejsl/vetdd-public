**Specific Causes:** Lumpy skin disease virus (genus Capripoxvirus; Neethling strain)

**Affects:** Cattle (imported thin-skinned breeds most susceptible); springbok; giraffe; buffalo; impala

**Primary Organ System:** Integumentary (Skin); Vascular

**Secondary Organ System:** Respiratory; Lymphoid; Reproductive; Musculoskeletal

**Transmission:** Biting insects (Stomoxys); ticks (R. appendiculatus, A. hebraeum, R. decoloratus); iatrogenic; saliva (drinking troughs)/milk; semen (present up to 42 days); animal movement

**Clinical Signs:**

- Many cases subclinical

- Biphasic fever

- Salivation; lacrimation; nasal discharge

- Conjunctivitis and corneal opacity

- Superficial lymph nodes enlarged

- Skin nodules appear before or during second fever

- Lesions resolve, become hard lumps, or ulcerate; necrotic skin sloughs off

- Swollen legs (oedema)

- Bulls: orchitis, temporary/permanent infertility

- Cows: udder lesions, mastitis; infertility

Peracute:

- Severe cases: upper respiratory tract affected (pneumonia)

**Pathological signs:**

- Integumentary: nodules in skin and subcutaneous tissue; multifocal circular necrotic areas on muzzle, respiratory tract, buccal cavity, GIT, reproductive organs, muscles; intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies in keratinocytes

- Lymphoid: generalised lymphadenopathy

- Vascular: vasculitis; thrombosis; infarction

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Multiply at site of entry and regional lnn. → primary viraemia to liver, spleen, lungs (Phase 1: 4–7 days) → secondary viraemia to skin → skin nodules erupt 7–19 days post-infection → vasculitis → necrosis/infarction

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- TEM; virus isolation; PCR; serology (ELISA; virus neutralisation test)

- Immunoperoxidase staining to visualise intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies

**Lab Findings**:

- Intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies in keratinocytes (vs. intranuclear in BoHV-2)

**Related symptom**:

- Skin nodules (sit-fast lesions attached to underlying tissue, unlike pseudo-LSD)

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes
