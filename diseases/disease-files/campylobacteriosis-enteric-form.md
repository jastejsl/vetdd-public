**Caused by:** Campylobacter coli & Campylobacter jejuni

**Affects:** Pigs, poultry, cattle, sheep, humans (zoonotic), canine

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal

**Transmission:** Faecal-oral route; contaminated food/water; undercooked poultry

**Other transmission routes:** Unpasteurised milk, polluted water; vertical transmission via cloaca in breeder hens; intensive broiler production; food chain via high-density poultry abattoir

**Clinical Signs:**

- Diarrhoea (with blood and inflammatory cells in faeces)

- Abdominal pain and fever

- Contributes to colitis in weaning pigs

- Mastitis (sheep)

**Pathological signs:**

- Intestinal tract: Cecal and colonic lesions with typhlocolitis

- Intestinal tract: Enlarged, fluid-filled ceca and proximal colons with thickened walls (swine)

- Lymph nodes: Ileocecocolic and mesenteric lymph nodes draining infected sites enlarged

- Intestinal crypts: Crypt abscesses and damage to crypt epithelium

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Bacterial motility via flagella (FlaA, FlaB) → mucus colonization in intestinal tract → adhesins facilitate attachment to host cell receptors → internalization (endocytosis) → intracellular survival in phagocytes → Cytolethal Distending Toxin production → epithelial damage → diarrhoea

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Isolation of organism from fresh faecal samples (transport media, 4°C)

- Culture at 42°C (thermophilic) with enrichment

- Microaerobic culture conditions required
  - Resistance to fluoroquinolones, tetracycline, kanamycin reported

- PCR-based methods

**Zoonosis:** yes | self-limiting
