**Specific Causes:** Bovine leukaemia virus

**Affects:** Cattle primarily; sheep can be infected from cattle (not between sheep)

**Primary Organ System:** Lymphoid/Haematopoietic

**Secondary Organ System:** Multicentric (liver, spleen, lymph nodes, etc.)

**Transmission:** Via transfer of lymphocytes: close contact and skin penetration; iatrogenic (dirty needles, ear tags, blood vaccines); mechanical spread via insect vectors; blood transfusions

**Clinical Signs:**

- Enzootic Bovine Leukosis (adult form, 5–8 years): 
  - enlargement of lymph nodes
  - weight loss
  - decreased milk production; 
  - persistent lymphocytosis; 
  - lymphoma; 
  - solid tumour masses or diffuse organ infiltration; 
  - can be subclinical

- Sporadic Bovine Leukosis 
  - Juvenile, (<3 years): non-contagious
  - Juvenile form (<6 months): generalised lymph node involvement
  - Thymic form (<2 years): thymic enlargement
  - Cutaneous form (1–3 years): nodular infiltration of lymphocytes in skin

**Pathological signs:**

- Lymphoid: multicentric lymphoma; lymph node enlargement; persistent B-cell lymphocytosis

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- No viraemia after infection → persistent humoral response → virus integrates into lymphocyte genome → chromosome abnormalities → cell transformation → B-cell lymphocytosis → lymphomas and multicentric organ invasion

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Clinical pathology (Lab Findings)

- Serology: ELISA or immunodiffusion

- PM examination

- PCR
